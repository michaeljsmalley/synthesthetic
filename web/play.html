How to play it

Three ways in, all at once if you like.

Mouse or touch

Click or tap a key or a button to press it. Drag a knob up or down, or turn it with the mouse wheel.

To hold a button while you play (SEQ, FX, ARP and the others), right-click it, or press and hold it on a touch screen: it stays down until you click it again. On a touch screen you can also hold it with one finger and tap keys with another.

Computer keyboard

Two rows of piano: Z to , with S D F H J L are F3 to F4, Q to I with 2 3 5 6 8 are G4 to G5. The white keys are also the 16 steps.

Space PLAY, Enter REC, Esc HOME, Shift SEQ, A ARP, G GLO, ← → OCT, ↑ ↓ SELECT, [ ] PRESETS.

MIDI keyboard

Press Connect MIDI and allow it. Your keyboard then plays the selected track, as the FM-1's own keys do.

Five things to try

  1. Press PLAY. On a first visit the demo is a song, about 80 seconds of deep house at 122 BPM in the FM-1's song mode: an intro, a verse, the chorus, the verse again, a bridge and the last chorus, then round again. STOP leaves the verse as a loop to play over. Hold SAVE to see the song: white keys 1 to 4 play its sections (A verse, B chorus, C bridge, D intro), key 13 switches between SONG and LOOP.

  2. Press HOME on the TRACKS screen. The visualiser fills the screen and the webcam shows the same picture. Turn SELECT for the next of the 31 modes, PRESETS for the palette, the four knobs to shape it.

  3. Hold SEQ and tap white keys to switch steps on and off for the selected track. OCT− and OCT+ page through the steps.

  4. Hold FX and press a white key for a punch-in effect while the groove plays. Let go and it's gone.

  5. Pick SCREEN, the last mode. The webcam then shows the FM-1's own display, ready to stream your workflow.

What runs here

The FM-1 on this page runs Synthesthetic's own firmware: the same C sources as the FM-1, compiled to WebAssembly and running in your browser's audio thread. The synth engines, the sequencer, the screens, the LEDs and the visualizers are the real ones. Only the hardware is stood in for: the screen is a canvas, the keys are your mouse, keyboard or MIDI, and the flash is this browser's storage.

Not here: USB, so the page shows the webcam picture instead of sending it, and the editor and installer cannot reach this FM-1. Uploading user samples and updating the firmware happen on a real FM-1.

The 3D FM-1 is adapted from FM1.quest by hericdk (GPL-3.0), and running the firmware in a page follows sabliran's sloop-web-emu. three.js draws the model.
